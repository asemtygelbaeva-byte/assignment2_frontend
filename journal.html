Observation / Landscape

A slower way
of looking.

A photograph starts before the camera comes out. It starts with noticing what makes a place feel different.

01 / Perspective

Start with the whole scene.

A wide landscape has a lot to say at once. Before deciding what belongs in the frame, look for a simple structure: a horizon, a reflection or a line that takes the eye into the distance.

In the lake photograph, the reflection repeats the mountain's shape. The shoreline separates the two halves, while the dark trees give the brighter peak a clear outline.

A different point of view can change a familiar place.

02 / Details

Then look a little closer.

The largest subject is not always the most interesting one. A rough surface, a repeated window or a bend in a path can become the centre of a photograph.

Try choosing just one detail. Move closer, leave some space around it and notice how much the image changes when the background becomes simpler.

03 / Light

Let the light do less.

Strong light creates clear edges. Cloudy light softens them. Neither is automatically better; each makes different details easier to see.

Compare the mountain, coast and city images in the collection. Their subjects differ, but so do their shadows, colours and contrast. Those choices help give each frame its own mood.
